Research

Research areas, methods, and current directions in human factors, physiological computing, human-state assessment, and human–AI interaction.

Research Overview

My research investigates how human states, experiences, and interactions with technology can be measured, modeled, and translated into better design and evaluation. I combine human-factors theory with multimodal physiological and behavioral data, controlled experiments, statistical modeling, and machine learning. The work is organized around three related areas: Human Factors & User Experience, Physiological Computing & Human-State Assessment, and Human–AI Interaction.

Research Background

My research has developed from engineering performance evaluation and applied ergonomics toward physiological computing and data-driven human-state assessment. Across these stages, the common goal has been to produce reliable and interpretable evidence about people that can inform safer, more usable, and more adaptive systems.

Research Approach

I approach research as an end-to-end process: defining a human-centered problem, designing the study, collecting multimodal physiological and behavioral data, preprocessing signals and extracting features, applying statistical or machine-learning methods, and translating the findings into interpretable evidence for system evaluation and design.

Human-centered question → Experimental design → Multimodal measurement → Signal and data processing → Statistical/ML analysis → Interpretation for design and evaluation

Human Factors & User Experience

I study how products, work systems, and interactive technologies affect human performance, safety, comfort, and experience. My work in this area spans physical ergonomics, usability evaluation, occupational risk assessment, automotive interfaces, and display-based interaction. Depending on the research question, I combine performance measures, questionnaires, eye tracking, EMG, and experimental or field-based evaluation.

This work reflects an applied approach to human factors: identifying measurable human requirements, evaluating systems under realistic conditions, and translating findings into evidence that can support product, interface, workplace, or evaluation-method development.

Topics and Methods

  • Physical ergonomics and musculoskeletal assessment
  • Usability and UX evaluation
  • Automotive interfaces and driver response
  • Display interaction and gaming performance
  • Occupational and industrial human factors
  • Eye tracking, EMG, questionnaires, and performance measures

Physiological Computing & Human-State Assessment

I use physiological and behavioral signals to assess human states that are difficult to observe directly. My research integrates EEG, ECG/HRV, EMG, eye tracking, and subjective measures to examine gaming-disorder risk, stress, affect, fatigue, and cognitive or behavioral responses.

The work covers the full analytical pipeline, including experimental design, signal acquisition, preprocessing, feature extraction, statistical modeling, and, when appropriate, machine-learning classification and explainability. A central theme is the identification of interpretable physiological parameters that can support quantitative assessment while remaining meaningful in the context of human behavior and experience.

Topics and Methods

  • EEG-based event and spectral analysis
  • ECG/HRV-based human-state assessment
  • EMG and muscle-fatigue analysis
  • Stress, affect, relaxation, and multisensory experience
  • Gaming-disorder risk and in-game behavior
  • Feature selection, classification, cross-validation, and SHAP

Human–AI Interaction

Current and Emerging Research Direction

My current and emerging research examines how AI-enabled systems can account for differences among users while remaining understandable, usable, and human-centered. I am particularly interested in combining physiological, behavioral, and subjective evidence to evaluate user responses to adaptive and explainable AI systems.

An ongoing project explores an adaptive XAI safety model for predicting and responding to sudden or unstable driving behaviors among users with diverse cognitive and sensory characteristics.

Topics and Methods

  • Human behavior in AI-enabled systems
  • Adaptive and explainable AI
  • User diversity and individual differences
  • Human-centered AI evaluation
  • Physiological and behavioral evidence for Human–AI Interaction
